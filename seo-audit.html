What Your Report Card Covers.

Every audit runs the same on-page checks a consultant would open with — weighted by how much each one actually matters to your rankings, then rolled into a single grade from A+ to F. Here is what goes into it, in plain English.

Content

How much substance the page gives Google to work with. We measure the readable word count, check whether your target keyword appears in the places that carry weight — the title, the H1, the opening copy — and flag keyword stuffing, which hurts more than it helps. Image alt text is checked too, with an honest note: it mostly helps image search and accessibility.

Technical

The signals that decide whether Google can trust and index the page at all: HTTPS, a valid canonical tag, meta robots directives (a stray noindex is an automatic F — the page simply cannot rank), a mobile viewport tag, structured data in JSON-LD, character encoding and a favicon. Boring, invisible, and the fastest category to fix.

Meta & Social

How the page looks before anyone clicks it. The title tag is one of the strongest on-page ranking signals, so length and presence are weighted heavily. The meta description does not affect rankings — we label that honestly — but it drives click-through rate. Open Graph and Twitter card tags decide whether shared links get a proper preview or a bare URL.

Structure

Whether the page is organised the way crawlers and screen readers expect: exactly one H1 that states the topic, a heading hierarchy that does not skip levels, internal links that help Google discover the rest of your site, outbound links to sources, and a declared page language. Small individually — together they decide how cleanly the page is understood.

Common Questions.

Is it really free?

Yes. Enter a URL, get a grade — no payment details, nothing to install. The analysis itself runs in your browser, on your device. One caveat for transparency: browsers can’t request other websites directly, so the public HTML of the URL you enter is fetched through a proxy relay service — only audit URLs you’re comfortable sharing (avoid private preview links or URLs containing tokens).

How is the grade calculated?

Each check carries a weight based on real-world impact — a missing title tag costs far more than a missing favicon — and the weighted results are normalized to a score out of 100. 95+ earns an A+, below 40 is an F. If you skip the optional keyword field, the keyword checks are simply removed and the remaining weights are redistributed, so you are never penalized for leaving it blank.

Why does my JavaScript site score low on content?

This tool reads the server HTML — the same first thing Googlebot fetches. If your content only appears after JavaScript runs, Google can still index it, but slower and less reliably. A near-empty result here is worth taking seriously: server-side rendering or pre-rendering usually fixes it.

Will fixing these issues put me at #1 on Google?

No single tool can promise that, and you should be suspicious of any that does. On-page fundamentals are the entry ticket: they stop your site from losing rankings it should already have. Beyond that, rankings depend on content quality, links and competition — Google’s own SEO starter guide is a good honest reference. When you want the whole job handled, that is what we do.
