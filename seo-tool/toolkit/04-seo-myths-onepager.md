# SEO Myths: What Actually Moves the Needle

**Position Xero** — SEO Client Toolkit · Client-facing · Last updated July 2026

SEO attracts more folklore than almost any other part of marketing. Here's the truth behind the ten myths we hear most often — and what we actually do for you instead.

---

**Myth 1: "Meta descriptions boost rankings."**
They don't. Google has confirmed meta descriptions aren't a ranking factor — and Google rewrites them for a large share of search results anyway. What they *do* influence is click-through rate: a compelling description wins more clicks from the rankings you already have.
*What we do instead:* write descriptions as ad copy — clear benefit, clear reason to click — and let rankings come from the page itself.

**Myth 2: "Put keywords in the meta keywords tag."**
Google confirmed publicly back in 2009 that it doesn't use the meta keywords tag in web ranking — and it had already been ignoring it for years before that. Filling it in does nothing except tell your competitors exactly which terms you're targeting.
*What we do instead:* put keyword effort where Google actually looks — titles, headings, body copy, and internal links.

**Myth 3: "Submitting a sitemap makes you rank higher."**
A sitemap helps Google *find* your pages faster — it's an indexing tool, not a ranking signal. Being indexed is the entry ticket, not the prize.
*What we do instead:* maintain a clean sitemap and Search Console setup so nothing gets lost, then focus ranking effort on content and links.

**Myth 4: "Alt text is a big ranking factor."**
Alt text matters mainly for image search and for accessibility (screen readers). Its effect on normal web rankings is minor. Stuffing keywords into alt attributes helps no one.
*What we do instead:* write honest, descriptive alt text — good for visually impaired visitors, good for image search, and it keeps you on the right side of Google's guidelines.

**Myth 5: "There's a magic keyword density percentage."**
There isn't, and Google's John Mueller has said as much repeatedly. Repeating a keyword to hit a percentage reads as stuffing — which can actively hurt you.
*What we do instead:* write for search *intent*. If the page fully answers what the searcher wanted, the keywords take care of themselves.

**Myth 6: "More pages = more traffic."**
Publishing hundreds of thin pages is now a fast route to a sitewide demotion. Google's spam policies explicitly target "scaled content abuse" — mass-produced pages (AI-generated or otherwise) made primarily to rank rather than to help people.
*What we do instead:* fewer, stronger pages. Every page we publish has a real audience, a real purpose, and something genuinely useful on it.

**Myth 7: "Older domains rank better."**
Google has said domain age itself isn't a ranking factor. Older sites often rank well because they've had years to earn links and content — that's correlation, not cause.
*What we do instead:* build the things age correlates with — authority, links, and a track record of helpful content — regardless of when your domain was registered.

**Myth 8: "SEO is one-and-done."**
Rankings decay. Competitors publish, algorithms update, links go stale, and content ages out. SEO is closer to fitness than to surgery — the results compound with consistency and fade with neglect.
*What we do instead:* ongoing monitoring, refreshing content that's slipping, and monthly reporting so you can see the compounding for yourself.

**Myth 9: "Running Google Ads improves your organic rankings."**
Google keeps ads and organic search completely separate — paying for ads neither helps nor hurts organic positions. Ads can be a smart complement, but they're not a shortcut to rankings.
*What we do instead:* treat paid and organic as separate channels with separate jobs, and use ad data (like which search terms convert) to inform organic strategy.

**Myth 10: "We guarantee you'll rank #1."**
No one controls Google's results — not us, not anyone. Google itself warns businesses to be wary of SEOs who guarantee rankings. Anyone promising #1 is selling something, and it usually isn't SEO.
*What we do instead:* guarantee the work and the transparency — a clear plan, honest reporting, and measurable progress on traffic and leads, not empty ranking promises.

## What actually moves the needle

- [ ] Content that fully answers what real customers are searching for
- [ ] A technically healthy site — fast, crawlable, mobile-friendly
- [ ] Titles and descriptions written to earn the click
- [ ] Links and mentions earned through genuinely useful content
- [ ] Consistency — reviewing, refreshing, and improving month after month
